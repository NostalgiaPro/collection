Top 10 NES games

May 13, 2026View on X
  1. 1
    Super Mario Bros. 3 Post

    The greatest game ever made. Not just on the NES, but of all time.

    More
    The world map, the suits, and the sheer volume of secrets changed the industry forever. If you think any other game deserves this spot, you’re just trying to be different for the sake of it.
  2. 2
    Adventures of Lolo Post

    Real gamers appreciate a puzzle. Lolo proves that you don't need a gun or a sword to have a "hardcore" experience.

    More
    It’s a relentless logic trap that makes you feel like a genius for five seconds before the next room humbles you again. HAL Laboratory at its absolute finest.
  3. 3
    Conquest of the Crystal Palace Post

    This is the best game you never played.

    More
    It combines platforming with an actual shop system and a dog companion that actually helps you. The visuals were years ahead of 1990, and the difficulty curve is exactly how games should be balanced.
  4. 4
    Contra Post

    The Konami Code is for people who can't handle a real challenge.

    More
    Playing Contra with the standard three lives is the only way to experience the pure adrenaline of the jungle. It’s the peak of 8-bit action, and nothing in the last thirty years has topped the feel of the Spread gun.
  5. 5
    Adventure Island Post

    Hudson Soft took the Wonder Boy formula and made it a punishing test of endurance.

    More
    Managing your hunger meter while dodging snails is more stressful than any modern survival horror game. It’s colorful, bright, and will absolutely break your spirit.
  6. 6
    Double Dragon II: The Revenge Post

    The controls are polarizing because they require a brain.

    More
    Having buttons mapped to the "left" and "right" attacks instead of "punch" and "kick" is brilliant design. The cyclone kick is the most rewarding move in the beat-em-up genre, period.
  7. 7
    X-Men Post

    Unfair? Yes.

    More
    Chaotic? Absolutely. But LJN’s X-Men is better than the "hidden gem" YouTubers want you to believe. If you couldn't beat it, you just didn't understand the character swap mechanic. It’s a brutal, unforgiving gauntlet that rewards persistence over luck.
  8. 8
    Duck Hunt Post

    The Zapper is the greatest peripheral ever made, and this game is the ultimate test of reflex.

    More
    We all tried to shoot that laughing dog, but the real tension was hitting those clay pigeons on Round 15. It’s pure, tactile arcade energy that modern VR still can't replicate.
  9. 9
    Super Mario Bros. 2 Post

    Stop calling it a "reskin" of Doki Doki Panic. This is the most experimental and creative Mario title in the entire 8-bit library.

    More
    Picking up enemies and throwing them is more satisfying than jumping on heads, and playing as Princess Peach was the original "easy mode" before it was cool.
  10. 10
    Popeye Post

    Most arcade ports on the NES were compromised, but Popeye is pixel-perfect.

    More
    It’s a masterclass in high-score chasing that requires more strategy than half the "open world" games released today. If you can't appreciate the perfection of the spinach mechanic, you aren't a real gamer.

Top 10 N64 games

May 23, 2026View on X
  1. 1
    Wave Race 64 Post

    The best game on the N64 doesn't have a single enemy in it.

    More
    In 1996 nobody had seen water move like this on a home console. The first time you raced, the waves shoved your ski around so convincingly it almost felt broken. Nothing on the PS1 came close. Nothing else on the N64 did either. No spectacle, no gimmick. Just the hardware doing something genuinely new.
  2. 2
    Perfect Dark Post

    Smarter enemies than Goldeneye, more weapons, a bigger campaign, and bots so you didn't need three friends on the couch.

    More
    It came out two years later and quietly beat Goldeneye at the exact thing Goldeneye is worshipped for. Only one game on this list beats it.
  3. 3
    Star Wars Episode I: Racer Post

    Better than the movie. And I'll say the thing nobody wants to: it's the best Star Wars game ever made.

    More
    The speed felt genuinely dangerous, the tracks were trying to kill you, and I'd sink entire afternoons into upgrading a pod racer for a podrace that lasted four minutes. People fight me on this constantly.
  4. 4
    1080° Snowboarding Post

    People don't take snowboarding games seriously so this one just gets skipped entirely.

    More
    The physics were real, the courses actually demanded something from you, and it looked incredible for the time. I put more hours into this than I'd ever admit publicly.
  5. 5
    Goldeneye 007 Post

    Okay. Number 5.

    More
    Yeah it mattered. It made the console shooter a real thing and a movie tie-in had no right being that good. I'm not arguing any of that. But mattering and being the best game on the system are two different things, and four games here clear it.
  6. 6
    Pilotwings 64 Post

    Launched alongside the console and everyone forgot it existed by Christmas.

    More
    No enemies, nothing to shoot, just Nintendo figuring out how to move through 3D space before anyone else had even solved the camera. I'll say it: as a launch game it holds up better than Mario 64, and that one always gets people.
  7. 7
    Killer Instinct Gold Post

    Everyone remembers Killer Instinct on SNES. Almost nobody remembers Gold even existed.

    More
    It was the arcade sequel, KI2, brought home nearly intact, and the N64 pulled it off better than a cartridge had any right to. Full announcer, the combo system intact, none of it watered down. It's the best fighting game on the console and it isn't close.
  8. 8
    San Francisco Rush: Extreme Racing Post

    Arcade racing on this console basically starts and ends with Rush.

    More
    The tracks were ridiculous, there were shortcuts hidden in spots you'd only find by accident, and the crashes were the entire point. Every modern racing game took that out and decided it was an improvement. It wasn't.
  9. 9
    Duke Nukem: Zero Hour Post

    Third person, time travel, and weirdly funny in a way the PC games never were.

    More
    Came out in 1999, sold to about nine people, and disappeared. I think it's better than half the shooters that get brought up in these threads.
  10. 10
    Turok: Rage Wars Post

    Everyone acts like Goldeneye was the only multiplayer game on the N64. It wasn't.

    More
    Turok: Rage Wars was four player and honestly meaner and faster than anything Rare did, it just had the worst possible timing, dropping right when Goldeneye was still the only thing anyone wanted to play at a sleepover. Nobody gave it a chance.

Killer Instinct is the better game and I don't think that's up for debate. Rare's prerendered sprites, the combo system, the soundtrack CD in the box. Primal Rage is my guilty pleasure. The reviews were rough and I'm not going to argue with any of it. Clay dinosaurs shot frame by frame, fatalities where they eat the little people cheering at the bottom of the screen. It's not a great game but it's absolutely a fun game. Which 1995 fighter did you like more?
