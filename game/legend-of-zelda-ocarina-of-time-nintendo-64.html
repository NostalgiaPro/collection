Honest and Unpopular Reviews No. 11 The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (N64) I'm just going to say it: This is the most overrated game ever made. It's a good game. I replayed it recently, start to finish, as an adult. That's where this review is coming from, because I don't think most of the people calling it the greatest game ever made have played it since they were kids. Go back to it now and the first thing that hits you is how much of this game is spent running across nothing. Hyrule Field is a big empty rectangle with a few Peahats and some holes in it, and you cross it constantly. And before you ever get out there you're stuck doing errands in Kokiri Forest with Navi talking the whole time, because you can't even get into the Deku Tree until you've got the sword and the shield. Forest Temple is great. Fire Temple is hallways. Then there's the Water Temple. It isn't hard. The puzzles are fine. The problem is the iron boots, which you need to sink and walk underwater, are an equipment item buried in a pause menu, so the dungeon becomes pause, equip boots, walk, pause, unequip boots, swim, over and over for an hour. Even Nintendo admits this one. Eiji Aonuma, who directed the dungeon and went on to run the entire Zelda series, publicly apologized for it years later, and the 3DS remake moved the boots to a touch screen button so you'd never have to open the menu. When the guy who built it says sorry, it's not just me being contrarian. The story is thinner than people remember too. Ganondorf gets everything he wants the second you pull the Master Sword, and then he basically disappears for the entire adult half of the game while you work down a checklist of five sages, one per dungeon, until the game decides you're allowed to fight him. There's no real second act. What people are actually remembering is the music, and to be fair, the music deserves it. Gerudo Valley, Song of Storms, the Lost Woods. Koji Kondo put one of the best soundtracks ever made on that cartridge and I won't argue with anyone about that. But a great soundtrack wrapped around a thin story is still a thin story. What Ocarina actually earned is Z-targeting. Before this game, nobody had really figured out how to fight in 3D. Ocarina solved it by letting you lock onto an enemy so the camera holds on them while you circle, strafe, and block. That system is basically in every 3D action game since. That's the real legacy, and it's a bigger one than the story or the world ever was. Majora's Mask is the better game and it isn't perfect either. Ocarina sits at number one because it got there first, and a perfect score from 1998 has been handed down ever since without anyone stopping to ask if it still holds up. I rate this one a 4 out of 5.
