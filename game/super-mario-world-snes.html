Honest and Unpopular Reviews No. 19 Super Mario World (SNES) For a lot of us this is the game that came in the box with the console. Ours showed up in 1993, and my mom and I sat there and played it the same way we'd played the NES games, except now I was looking at colors the NES couldn't do and I couldn't get over it. I still catch myself humming the music to this game. It's one of the best games ever made and it's still not the best Mario game. Super Mario Bros. 3 holds that title. What I love about this game is how much of it is hidden but not to the point where it becomes frustrating. Just enough to keep things interesting. There are levels with a second exit you'd never find just beating them normally, and finding one opens up parts of the map you didn't know existed, so the map itself turns into the thing you're playing. Then there's Yoshi. Everybody remembers him as the thing that takes a hit for you, and that's the least interesting part of him. What he really does is change how you get through a level based on what's in his mouth. A red shell and he's spitting fire, a blue one and he's flying. The only negative thing I can say about Super Mario World is Mario feels slippery. But once you're used to it, you stop noticing. Ultimately, this was the last truly great Mario game. The drop off from this to Super Mario 64 was massive, despite it being the most important game on the console. I rate Super Mario World a 5 out of 5.
