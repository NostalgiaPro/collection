Overrated Game of the Day! Final Fantasy VII (PS1) Final Fantasy VII shows up near or at the top of best game ever made lists constantly and I've never understood how it got there. I bought it in 2001 because guys I worked with at KFC wouldn't stop telling me it was the greatest thing ever made. That's literally the only reason why I bought it. The story isn't great. A lot of the Final Fantasy die-hards describe it like it's some untouchable piece of writing, and I played the whole thing waiting for the part that was supposed to floor me. It never showed up. The game was boring. Finishing it felt like an assignment I had to get through rather than a game I wanted to keep playing. Especially when I had Gran Turismo, Mega Man Legends, Ready 2 Rumble, and Tomorrow Never Dies sitting right there next to the PS1 that were all infinitely more enjoyable to play. Calling Final Fantasy VII overrated isn't a hot take. It's the truth.

Honest and Unpopular Reviews No. 15 Final Fantasy VII (PS1) I've played Final Fantasy VII, I've played the remake, and both times I came away thinking the same thing. This game bored me. Midgar is the best part and it's over in a few hours. After that you're on a world map walking in a direction until the screen does the swirl and drops you into another fight you didn't ask for. The fights are a menu. You pick attack, you watch it happen, you pick it again. Summoning Knights of the Round takes over a minute and there's no skip button. That's not gameplay, that's a screensaver with a damage number at the end. People call this the greatest story ever told in a game. Most of it is characters standing still while text scrolls, and the pacing after disc one never recovers. The materia system is the one thing I'll defend without hesitation. Being able to strip a character down and rebuild them into something completely different was ahead of anything else on the console, and nothing since has done it better. Three discs, forty hours, and the parts I actually remember all happened in the first city. The remake had a chance to fix it. Instead they took Midgar, the one section that works, and stretched it into a full game. I rate this one a 3 out of 5.
